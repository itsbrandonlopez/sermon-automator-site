Monday's hidden shift

The sermon is finished. The real work is just starting.

Nobody puts this on the calendar, and it never gets easier. Every service produces the same second job: transcribe it, describe it, tag it, chapter it, cut it down, caption it — all before it can do the one thing it was made to do, which is be found.

  • Transcribing a 90-minute service by hand, or babysitting a tool that half-does it.
  • Writing a description from scratch, in a voice that has to sound like your church and not like a chatbot.
  • Counting tag characters by hand, because YouTube's own limit doesn't forgive a guess.
  • Re-typing chapter markers you already placed once, in Resolve, an hour ago.
  • Doing the whole thing again for the sermon-only cut, because it isn't the same video.
  • Watching a good clip idea disappear because there was no time left in the week to go find it.

Why this part isn't optional

You already did the hard part.

The camera work, the mix, the message itself — that's finished the moment the service ends. It's also the expensive part, and it's already paid for. What happens in the next ten minutes is what decides whether any of it reaches past the room it was preached in.

A great sermon with no description, no chapters, and a guessed-at title reaches the same households it always has. The same sermon, described and tagged well, is what someone three miles away finds at 11pm on a Tuesday, searching for a church because they need one.

That's not a growth hack. It's the difference between a video that exists and a video that's doing its actual job — reaching the people in your city who are already looking for exactly what you filmed on Sunday.
